## Related work and research positioning

Delay-aware vehicle control and supervisory filtering are established research directions. Kalaria, Lin and Dolan study computation and actuation delays in autonomous driving and racing using delay estimation, steering-actuator dynamics, delay-shifted prediction and buffered control. Their framework includes robust tube MPC and a constrained guard for an MPC-imitation controller. The uploaded manuscript reports Gazebo non-racing and CARLA racing experiments, with improved lap times and fewer boundary violations when delay compensation is enabled. Its constraint reasoning depends on disturbance, model and estimator assumptions; the reported racing tables do not supply repeated-trial uncertainty. This is the closest vehicle/computation-delay application comparison among the five sources examined here, rather than an exhaustive ranking of prior work. [P1: Kalaria2022DelayAwareV1](../research/primary-papers/kalaria.md), §§III–IV, PDF pp.3–10.

Predictive safety filtering is a second foundation. Wabersich and Zeilinger formulate a model-based filter that minimally modifies proposed learning inputs while maintaining state/input constraints in probability under model-confidence, stabilizability, terminal-safety and initial-feasibility assumptions. Their simulated quadrotor study reports constraint satisfaction during 240 learning episodes. Training already occurs through the filter and its objectives penalize intervention, so execution through a supervisor and intervention-aware learning are not new principles introduced by LANS. Tearle et al. specialize predictive filtering to vehicle racing, using backup trajectories, terminal sets and input-rate regularization. They demonstrate human assistance and imitation learning on a 1:28-scale physical vehicle with nominal 80 Hz sampling. Their implementation softens track/terminal constraints and explicitly qualifies invariance across curvature changes. Neither that nominal sampling rate nor LANS's control-rate target constitutes a hard-deadline certificate. [P3: Wabersich2021PredictiveFilterV4](../research/primary-papers/wabersich.md), §§4–5, pp.5–12; [P2: Tearle2021RacingFilterV1](../research/primary-papers/tearle.md), Eq.(5), §§V–VI, pp.3,5–8.

Formal supervision under delayed information is also established. Cano Córdoba et al. synthesize shields for finite safety games with FIFO-delayed observations, a declared worst-case delay bound and latent-action history. Their CARLA examples report collision avoidance in the tested scenarios, including car-example delays up to 1.5 seconds, while acknowledging occasional empty strategies from abstraction discretization in the pedestrian example. These are game-based safety mechanisms under stated assumptions, distinct from rejecting a stale learned residual while a local LQR tracker continues. [P4: CanoCordoba2023DelayedShielding](../research/primary-papers/cano.md), PDF pp.2–5 / proceedings pp.81–84.

For learning under communication latency, the reviewed May 2026 version of Tabrizian et al.'s DAROM combines SAC, a delay-aware representation of neighboring-vehicle observations, action history and observed age, and a stopping-distance safety controller. Its SUMO Hard setting reports 99.80 ± 0.23% successful merges, 0.00% observed collisions and 0.20 ± 0.23% no-merge outcomes under an observation-delay envelope up to two seconds. Actions are explicitly immediate and ego state is current. This is therefore a different delay channel from LANS's asynchronous residual worker. The reported ± statistic is not explicitly defined, and other table/prose inconsistencies require clarification before reproduction. These results support task-specific effectiveness, not a formal collision-risk guarantee or a direct numerical ranking against LANS tracking RMSE. [P5: Tabrizian2026DAROMV5](../research/primary-papers/tabrizian.md), §§II–V, PDF pp.3–8, Table III p.10.

Against this background, LANS asks a narrower empirical question: **under explicit execution delays, does a learned steering residual provide realized tracking value beyond a strong LQR and simple nonlearned corrections admitted through the same supervisor, and what heading and steering costs accompany that value?** The implementation combines observation-age checks, bounded fusion, predicted fused-command benefit and local LQR fallback. Its contribution must be assessed through controlled comparisons and execution evidence, rather than attributed to the novelty of combining learning, classical control and a guard.

The latest LANS evidence does not yet establish a balanced learned advantage. Revision13 replicates a nonlearned fixed-correction gain of 9.131% on the original positive-orientation roads. On an expanded mirrored confirmation grid, fixed corrections reduce mean lateral RMSE by 7.049%, compared with 4.126% for the frozen supervised student, while increasing steering-rate RMS by 36.543% and total movement by 48.823% versus LQR. Three predefined balanced objectives reduce movement but worsen tracking, so no candidate advances to reserved/native/learning stages. These results identify a tracking/smoothness and learning-attribution problem; they do not show superiority to the reviewed papers. [Revision13 evidence](../../integrations/webots/results/revision13-balanced/interpretation.md).

LANS currently supplies empirical bounds, survival, fallback and timing measurements, without an equivalent formal safety certificate, validated physical vehicle, or guaranteed deadlines. The separate real-worker study records strict 10 ms timing failure and a causal-feature limitation in the original student. Future improvement therefore requires a balanced controller, causally available learned inputs, fresh confirmation data and a feasible matched published-method comparison. [Timing evidence](../research/realtime-latency-2026-10-26/README.md).

## Citation and result-use rules

- Cite **Tabrizian v5, 27 May 2026** for the reviewed DAROM architecture and results; do not attribute them to the original 2024 version without reading it.
- Cite the inspected Kalaria title including **“and Racing”** and its v1 identifier. Its TIV header does not verify a TCST 2024 entry; its own preliminary-work reference is a distinct bibliographic lead.
- For Tearle's learning illustration, write **“later DAgger iteration”**. The v1 body says fifth, while Figure 6 labels fourth.
- Keep probabilistic theorem assumptions, softened implementations and empirical zero-event counts distinct. Do not call every reviewed guard a formally certified safety filter.
- Keep the historical revision12 representative-trace percentages separate from revision13 full-grid measurements. The large replicated LANS gain is nonlearned.
- Do not compare lap-time percentages, merging success, episode counts or different delay channels as a common leaderboard. No reviewed method has yet been reproduced under matched LANS conditions.

The five-source review narrows the positioning but is not a systematic novelty search. Johannink's residual-RL and Ramstedt's random-delay primary sources, among other relevant work, remain to be reviewed separately.
