### 4. Predicted improvement is not realized improvement or a safety guarantee — medium/high scientific priority

The frozen gate predicts with nominal linear 80k/80k tires, 10 ms Euler steps, local curvature, a 50 ms command hold and then LQR continuation (`revision2/gating.py:7`). The actual surrogate uses nonlinear tire forces, changing geometry and actuator dynamics. The diagnostic is appropriately a paired cloned-state simulation, with true state used only offline (`revision5/audit.py:30`, `:92`).

Recounting all 15,648 sampled state/candidate rows reproduces 38/664 false accepts for legacy PPO and 77/451 for A100k, using the predefined >1% realized-harm threshold; corresponding cost false rejects are 98 and 117 (`results/revision5-audit/report.md:64`). These are correlated shadow decisions along LQR trajectories, not independent failure probabilities, deployed-hybrid safety rates, or all possible beneficial actions. Even the “realized” rollout is a 200 ms counterfactual with prescribed LQR continuation, not the full future policy trajectory.

The separate nonlinear model bank uses declared fixed uncertainty values, not hidden episode parameters (`revision5/robust_gate.py:15`, `:33`, `:47`). It is a sensible diagnostic and its contract tests pass. Its 144 episodes have mixed/slightly worse aggregate scores and neither policy satisfies both-seed stress improvement (`results/revision5-robust/report.md:20`). The explicit decision not to promote it is correct. Adding model complexity has not solved the research problem.

### 5. Timing evidence is conditional and correctly separated in the modern path — medium priority

The surrogate computes inference synchronously and separately simulates packet availability (`revision2/run.py:45`; `revision4/evaluation.py:22`). In native Webots, a spawned process runs actual inference with one job in flight, drop-while-busy, and nonblocking control-loop polling (`research/worker.py:25`, `:33`, `:37`). A configured 20 ms delay is a minimum simulation-clock release delay, not a measured 20 ms end-to-end deadline. Polling, CPU contention and simulator progress also affect observed age. Native runs deliberately use wall and simulation clocks for different purposes.

The corrected-root measurement contains 1,000 actual synchronous normalized-policy inferences: median 0.181093 ms, p99 0.388568 ms (`results/controller-remediation/inference.json`; `tools/controller_review/benchmark.py:13`). This is a small smoke model on that cloud CPU. It times `PolicyBundle.predict`, not the complete estimator/gate/control/actuator stack, and does not establish an automotive frequency, WCET, schedulability, or equivalence to the original 203 µs laptop claim. Current descriptions largely state these limits accurately.

## Why LQR remains strong, and what ARIS concludes scientifically

The baseline is not merely a naïve fixed gain. It uses speed-scheduled Riccati feedback, development-selected common Q/R, a Kalman estimate, curvature feedforward and nonzero turning equilibrium (`research/comparison.py:8`, `tracking.py:5`). The tested paths are known and largely favorable to model-based tracking. Current mean errors already leave relatively little absolute error for PPO to remove.

The learned side has several documented limitations: original training/deployment mismatch; small effective authority after clipping and ≤0.5 blending; sparse accepted actions; reduced physical exploration after range matching; nominal predictor bias; and a short-horizon training objective that is not episode RMSE. Historical actor and baseline inputs also differ: the actor gets noisy speed and curvature previews, while old LQR/gate use scheduled speed and local curvature (`core.py:83`; `revision2/run.py:44`). These are concrete mechanisms and confounds, not proof of which one dominates. Revision5 repairs several interfaces but has no competent new trained policy yet.

The research contribution currently looks like a carefully documented empirical integration/negative-result study: small residual gains, reliable simulated fallback, and evidence that several plausible fixes do not help. That is useful. It is insufficient, on its own, for a strong general ML or control novelty claim or a convincing top-tier paper claiming a new broadly superior algorithm. A venue-specific related-work and citation audit was not performed here. A defensible paper would foreground measured tradeoffs and attribution, and state the narrow simulator/task scope.

## Strengths and remaining limits

Strengths include clear separation of root and isolated research plants, archived source snapshots, retained failures and negative results, explicit action units, fixed failure-tail scoring, seed-level summaries, predeclared spending/promotion screens, exact severe-delay fallback checks, and candid reporting of invalid/recovered/interrupted runs. The latest README and research reports are markedly more defensible than the original manuscript. The saved diagnostics and new consistency checks substantiate these strengths.

This review does not certify every program, binary or manuscript statement. There was no fresh native simulator run, full checkpoint retraining, exhaustive semantic read of all 209 primary files, manual reading of every duplicate/archive member, formal stability proof, hardware WCET audit, external citation verification, or visual comparison of every figure. Some historical archives provide source and tables but incomplete runnable packages. Coverage is explicit in `coverage.md`; unsupported or unread areas are not silently assigned PASS.

**Decision:** retain the current small-gain/fallback claims with their qualifications; reject unrestricted repository-wide or original-paper superiority/safety claims; complete the low-cost evidence cleanup first; keep full training paused until a new, controlled small experiment provides a reason to proceed.
