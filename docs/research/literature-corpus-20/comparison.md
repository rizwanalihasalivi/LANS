# LANS versus the supplied literature: overlap, evidence and a candidate gap

Reviewed 2026-10-28 (Asia/Shanghai). Scope: the 20 individually supplied PDFs archived in this folder, not the inaccessible earlier ZIP and not all published literature. Page numbers below are **PDF page ordinals**, including front matter. These are source-reported methods and experiments, not independently reproduced results.

## Scope and review depth

The archive contains **20 files, 19 distinct byte sequences and 18 distinct works**: P09/P20 are byte-identical Kalaria copies; P04/P08 are preprint/publisher versions of the same Wabersich–Zeilinger work. Both versions remain preserved. The manifest records size, SHA-256 and page count. A larger file count must not inflate the independent evidence count.

All files were successfully downloaded and text-extracted. This is a targeted methods/experiments/limitations comparison, with closer examination of the most relevant switching, delay, timing and recovery passages. It is **not** a line-by-line proof audit, visual validation of every plotted value, code reproduction, or exhaustive citation search. Earlier detailed reviews of the five previously supplied works are reused and linked below. Text extraction of some IEEE tables is incomplete; no new quantitative claims depend on their unreadable cells. Publisher identity is taken from the supplied documents; external publisher authentication was not performed.

LANS here means the user's **original proposal**: full-command PPO normally has control authority; a separately executing LQR receives fresh local state and takes over without waiting for stalled PPO; obsolete commands are rejected; a defined recovery rule returns authority to PPO. Historical LQR-default residual experiments in the repository do not validate this original architecture. This review does not alter controllers, training or the specification.

## Main conclusion

**The corpus does not establish one common defect that all papers have and LANS already solves.** It contains counterexamples to several previously suggested blanket gaps:

- Advanced learned control, verified backup, state-dependent switching and return to the advanced controller already appear in **Simplex-Drive (P07)**.
- Burst deadline misses, actuator hold/zero choices, execution kill/skip choices, and the recovery needed for stability and performance are already studied in **P16**.
- Measured hardware timing and a faster safety loop alongside slower MPC/PPO are already demonstrated in **P19**. P01 also reports target-hardware turnaround times and field experiments.
- Delay compensation, bounded/smooth steering, training with a supervisor, robustness to observation delay, and multi-objective evaluation are distributed across the other papers.

Therefore neither “PPO + LQR + threshold,” “state-aware switching,” “measure deadline misses,” nor “smooth recovery” alone is established novelty. Merely combining existing ingredients does not automatically create a new algorithm.

## Closest prior art changes the proposed contribution

**Simplex-Drive must be the architectural starting point**, ahead of a generic comparison only with the earlier five papers. Its p.4 definition includes one-step advanced-control reachability, infinite-horizon backup safety and safety after returning to backup. Eq.(1) already switches back from BC to AC when the state is recoverable. Theorem 1 includes Δt > T_AC and Δt > T_BC; its proof relies on BC executing during the next management interval. A LANS paper must explain what it changes when computation availability violates that assumption, while retaining a justified backup response bound. Replacing ORCA with LQR is not by itself an algorithmic contribution or an inherited safety proof.

**P16 already joins timing faults to plant performance.** It models a burst of m missed jobs followed by n successful jobs, compares execution and actuation choices, and evaluates recovery cost. Its p.19 explicitly notes ideal, zero-cost rollback for Kill and a linear-system limitation of the performance analysis. It also states applicability to time-varying/local nonlinear analysis. LANS should use this work as a baseline/foundation instead of claiming to introduce burst recovery or the importance of held commands.

**P19 defeats a blanket “no actual timing evidence” argument.** Table 11 reports mean fast-loop latency 9.6 ms, standard deviation 1.1 ms, maximum observed 14.5 ms and zero observed misses against 20 ms. These are finite-trace measurements, not certified WCET. Its pp.23–24 explicitly reserve interface-fault testing and stronger production determinism for deployment. LANS would need fault-conditioned, equal-resource comparisons; ordinary timestamps and a fast fallback loop are not enough to distinguish it.

## A candidate question worth investigating

> Can an actuator-aware, uncertainty-bounded handover rule jointly determine how long a full-command learned controller may remain authoritative and when a separately scheduled backup must execute, while preserving a defined recoverable region during computation bursts and return to learned control?

This is a **candidate intersection not established as a complete solution in the inspected passages**, not a claim of universal absence or a common defect of all 18 works. State recoverability, scheduling, actuator modeling and bumpless transfer each have extensive prior art. More primary review of timed/Simplex variants, weakly-hard control, control–scheduling co-design, mixed-criticality isolation, networked control and bumpless transfer could eliminate or substantially narrow this candidate.

The potentially substantive work is a constructive rule and its conditions, not a new name:

1. Define an augmented state containing tracking state, actual steering/actuator state, current held command, observation age, controller mode and command generation identifier. Include bounded model/estimation uncertainty and actuator rate/lag limits.
2. Compute a conservative time margin over which the **actually applied** command/transition keeps the reachable set inside a region recoverable by the specified backup. Do not equate distance to a lane boundary with recoverability.
3. Include bounds for monitor detection, scheduling, backup computation and command delivery. In a simple sequential timing model, waiting is admissible only if waiting plus these response components fits inside the recoverability margin. If activities overlap, use their actual causal timing model rather than double-counting or assuming independence.
4. Validate the complete takeover and recovery trajectory, including steering-rate constraints; a smooth blend is not automatically safe. When no feasible transition or response bound exists, report the guarantee as unavailable and invoke a separately specified contingency. LQR alone does not promise recovery from arbitrary states.
5. Treat asynchronous command ownership separately from control-law design: single authoritative publisher, generation/sequence rejection and original observation timestamps. These are correctness requirements, not assumed novel algorithms.

Any guarantee is conditional on the stated uncertainty bounds, initial recoverability, sensing/actuation availability, and backup/monitor execution guarantees. A separate Python process or CPU affinity alone does not prove isolation from shared memory, bus, kernel, power or sensor failures. The scope must name which common failures are covered and which are excluded.

## How to test whether the candidate adds anything

Freeze the protocol before evaluating held-out cases. Preserve full-command PPO as the normal controller; do not silently substitute the historical residual architecture.

| Comparison | Required fairness / purpose |
|---|---|
| Tuned LQR alone and PPO alone | Establish whether normal PPO authority has a useful performance/efficiency benefit. If LQR is consistently better at the intended task, the extra complexity needs another demonstrated justification. |
| Competent fixed-deadline switching | Same backup, sensing, stale-command rejection, scheduling resources and reasonable recovery. Do not cripple the baseline by waiting synchronously for PPO. |
| State/recoverability-based switching | Adapt Simplex-style reasoning transparently; identify changed dynamics and do not inherit its original theorem. |
| Deadline-burst handling baseline | Compare sensible hold and execution-drop/skip policies using P16's lessons; document applicability and differences. |
| Actuator-aware rule without resource-aware timing, and timing-aware rule without actuator transition reasoning | Isolate the value of each candidate component. |
| Joint proposed rule | Same controller models, paths, initial conditions, CPU budget and faults as the baselines. |

Inject isolated stalls, bursts, worker crash/restart, late and out-of-order completions, and controlled shared CPU contention. Vary curvature, speed, friction and actuator response within declared bounds. Keep stale perception tests separate from PPO computation stalls. The plant must continue evolving while the controller is stalled; a synchronous simulation that pauses the plant can conceal the fault.

Record end-to-end observation-to-actuation age, monitor/backup deadline misses, takeover time, stale-command acceptance (target zero), transition steering rate/jerk, lateral/heading error, constraint violations, recovery duration, and fraction of unnecessary backup interventions. Report latency distributions/maxima and denominators; zero observed misses is not a universal guarantee. Native Webots can validate vehicle behavior, while real timing under load requires wall-clock execution and an appropriate plant/HIL timing arrangement.

The falsifiable target is **better fault tolerance or less conservative authority use at equal resources and equivalent safety conditions**. It is not an arbitrary 5–10% RMSE target. If a well-tuned fixed switch achieves the same result, report no demonstrated advantage. If the research stops at an implementation and fault-injection benchmark, describe the contribution as empirical systems evaluation, not a new safety theorem.

## Opening-report wording

> Existing work provides learned vehicle controllers, predictive safety filters, recoverability-based supervisory switching, deadline-miss recovery analysis, and measured multi-rate safety execution. LANS will investigate the remaining integration question of how actuator-state-dependent recoverability and bounded backup response time should jointly govern authority transfer and recovery for a full-command learned vehicle controller under computational faults. The study will compare against competent fixed-deadline and state-aware supervisors under matched resources. Novelty and benefit remain hypotheses to be tested; no universal prior-art absence or unconditional safety guarantee is claimed.

This replaces the unsupported claim that every related paper assumes instantaneous or always-available safety computation. P07, P09, P16 and P19 must be treated as close prior art. Broader primary-source review remains necessary before asserting that the proposed joint mechanism is new.
