# Five uploaded primary papers: verified LANS comparison

Reviewed 26 October 2026 (Asia/Shanghai). The five supplied PDFs were read locally, including methods, assumptions and experiments; relevant result tables/figures were visually checked. This resolves the access barrier for **these five versions**, not the broader literature search. Paper-reported results have been checked against the PDFs, not independently reproduced. LANS reference: `3b0d08491e8c889da1efcae47fa07f21bac4a5fa`, containing the completed revision13 campaign.

**The user's main positioning is supported:** Kalaria is the closest vehicle/computation-delay application comparison among this supplied set; Tearle and Wabersich supply predictive-filter foundations, Cano Córdoba supplies formal delayed-observation supervision, and Tabrizian supplies a recent learning/observation-delay comparison. Their tasks, information assumptions and metrics do not establish a numerical ranking against LANS. The review additionally finds substantial prior overlap in **training with the filter, penalizing intervention, input-rate smoothing, imitation learning, and explicit age/action-history inputs**. Those ingredients alone are not LANS novelty.

## Safety, delay and performance must remain separate

| Evidence dimension | What the uploaded sources establish | What LANS currently establishes |
|---|---|---|

Kalaria relies on tube/CBF assumptions and bounded uncertainties. | A nominal predicted-benefit check, tested bounds/survival and exact stale fallback. | Delayed information/control | Kalaria predicts computation/actuation delay; Cano reasons over stale environmental information; DAROM delays neighbor observations while ego state is current and actions are immediate. | The headline 100 ms case delays residual-worker availability while local LQR continues; sensor-stress/delay cases are separately defined. ## Important prior overlap beyond the supplied comparison

1. **Train with the supervisor and penalize interventions:** Wabersich already does both. The pendulum objective penalizes proposed-versus-filtered input differences; the quadrotor objective strongly penalizes intervention, and the final reported policy leaves the filter inactive (PDF pp.10–12). These are not new LANS principles.
2. **Make supervisory commands smoother:** Tearle Eq.(5), PDF p.3, adds input-rate regularization while assigning much greater weight to following the desired input. This is relevant to LANS's failed weighted balancing experiment, but it is a different constrained objective and supplies no LANS guarantee.
3. **Learn from a classical teacher:** Kalaria's MPC-imitation network (p.9) and Tearle's MPCC/DAgger policy (p.7) already pair learned commands with a model-based guard. LANS's current retained learner is also supervised; calling its result a new PPO gain would be incorrect.
4. **Represent delay and history explicitly:** DAROM augments state with age and action history (pp.3–5); Cano uses latent-action memory for delayed observations (pp.2–3). LANS's execution bookkeeping remains valuable implementation/evaluation work, not an exclusive concept.
5. **Study interventions and component attribution:** Cano compares corrective-action heuristics and interventions; DAROM ablates encoder inputs and its guard. It would be false to claim prior literature never studies intervention or component contributions. The specific same-gate LQR-residual fixed-correction comparison is a narrower difference in these inspected experiment sets.

## Current LANS numbers supersede the historical snapshot

The user's 1,080-episode/4.528% student/9.122% fixed result and representative-trace +4.78% heading/+14.1% steering-rate figures are the **historical revision12 distribution and trace subset**. They should not be mixed with revision13 full-grid metrics.

The [revision13 confirmation](../../../integrations/webots/results/revision13-balanced/confirmation/report.md) reports 2,160/2,160 survivors. On its original-positive subset, the fixed correction repeats **9.131%** improvement. Across the expanded mirrored grid, same-gate fixed signs each improve lateral RMSE by **7.049%**, the lateral-gated student by **4.126%**, and the legacy-gated student by **1.926%**. Fixed correction raises heading RMSE **0.833%**, actuator-rate RMS **36.543%** and movement **48.823%** across the full grid.

The separate [balanced development](../../../integrations/webots/results/revision13-balanced/development/report.md) completes 5,616/5,616 survivors. Light/medium/strong fixed profiles reduce movement but have **1.395%, 4.504% and 9.443% higher lateral RMSE than LQR**. No candidate passes; reserved, native, teacher and PPO stages remain untriggered. The [full interpretation](../../../integrations/webots/results/revision13-balanced/interpretation.md) records all limitations and independent metric verification. The total 7,776 episodes are matched/correlated comparisons, not 7,776 independent policy seeds or a safety certificate.

## Defensible contribution and next use of the literature

The focused research question remains:

> Under explicitly delayed execution, when does a learned steering residual add realized tracking value beyond a strong LQR and simple nonlearned corrections passed through the same supervisor, and what heading/steering costs accompany that value?

The present contribution is an auditable implementation and controlled empirical investigation of that question. The current strongest lateral gain is nonlearned; a balanced learned advantage is **not established**. Freshness, predicted fused-command benefit, fallback and matched attribution controls define the study, but five selected sources cannot establish universal novelty, superiority or a first-of-its-kind combination.

For the opening report, use P1 as the application comparison, P2/P3 for predictive filtering, P4 for formal delayed supervision, and exact P5 v5 for recent age/history-aware learning. Cite the mechanism and its assumptions, rather than inventing a missing capability in all prior work. The [ready-to-use related-work text](../../opening-report/verified-related-work.md) follows this distinction.

For future experiments, these sources support inspecting the existing LANS traces for intervention switching and objective conflicts, separating tracking preservation from secondary smoothing, and ensuring future learned inputs contain only causally observable age/history. They do **not** justify opening the failed revision13 reserved stage, increasing training budget, assuming a GRU solves the problem, or promoting a rule to a formal safety filter. A new design needs its own predeclared development protocol.

Still open: primary review of previously identified Johannink/residual-RL and Ramstedt/random-delay leads (not among these uploads), broader recent-literature search, final publisher metadata where needed, and a matched reproduction of the closest feasible published controller. A method adapted to LANS must document changed dynamics, sensing, constraints, delay channels, actuator limits and compute budgets; it cannot inherit the original paper's guarantee or result numbers automatically. No published method was implemented or benchmarked in this document review.
